# Pair screen v2 — full NSE F&O stock universe, run 2026-08-31 (as-of close 2026-08-28)

**Result: 0 pairs admitted out of 347 candidates, at N = ₹10 lakh and at N = ₹25 lakh per
leg alike. No entry candidates, empty watchlist, at both sizes.** Widening the universe
from 49 Nifty 50 names to 200 F&O names moved both binding constraints and broke neither:

- **Horizon.** The fastest-reverting candidate now has an OU half-life of **5.00 sessions**
  against the ≤ 5 bar — against **6.5** over the Nifty 50 — and **36** candidates are inside
  10 sessions against **4**. One pair (NMDC/SAIL) passes the horizon gate, by 0.0007
  sessions. Read that improvement against honesty item 13: a minimum over 347 noisy
  estimates is smaller than a minimum over 54 even when nothing has really improved.
- **Significance.** The best cointegration p improves 11× (0.027 → **0.0024**) but *m* rises
  from 54 to 347, so the rank-1 Benjamini–Hochberg bar falls from 0.0019 to **0.000288**.
  The best pair is 8.3× over its bar, against 14× in iteration 1 — closer, still rejected, and
  the full step-up rejects nothing at any rank.
- **Capital.** Unchanged in character. The median pair still needs **₹22.0 lakh** per leg to
  express its hedge ratio to 10 %, against ₹22.5 lakh over the Nifty 50. Quadrupling the
  universe did not find pairs that fit a small book.

This is a **screen**, not a backtest. It fits a formation window and evaluates admission
inequalities. It runs no trading rule, opens no position, and computes no return. Nothing
below is evidence that any pair is or would have been profitable.

## 2. A vendor data defect the wider universe exposed

Six names came back with a session's candle repeated. For five (`CANBK`, `GMRAIRPORT`,
`LTF`, `TMPV`, `UNITDSPR`) the repeats are identical and carry no information, so they
collapse. For **`MOTHERSON` the repeats disagree**: two complete price series share the
symbol across 162 sessions at a ratio of 1.49991–1.49992 (a 2:3 adjustment plus tick
rounding) — an unadjusted line and a
corporate-action-adjusted one. There is no basis on this host for choosing between them, and
fitting a spread on the wrong one would fit an artefact, so the name is **dropped and
reported** rather than silently resolved to whichever row sorts first.

This matters beyond one name. It shows the daily endpoint does not guarantee a
split/bonus-adjusted series, and `MOTHERSON` is visible only because the API returned *both*
lines. A name returning only an unadjusted line would show a one-session level jump and
nothing else. The screen now flags names whose formation window contains a log return above
0.4 as possible unadjusted corporate actions: **none in this window**. That is a weak check —
it catches a 2:1 split, not a 5 % bonus — and it is reported as a flag, not a drop, because
a threshold on realised returns would make the universe itself data-dependent.

## 3. Pre-filter

PCA on standardised daily log returns, 15 components; the first explains **32.0 %** of
variance, the next four 4.1 / 3.4 / 2.2 / 1.9 %. Per-name loading vectors clustered with
OPTICS.

The degeneracy test was pre-registered in code (`screen.py`: fewer than 2 clusters, or a
largest cluster over 60 % of names, or over 70 % noise). OPTICS returned **19 clusters,
largest 6.5 % of names, noise 45.0 % — not degenerate, so the OPTICS branch ran.** 110 of
the 200 names fall in a cluster; the other 90 are noise and contribute no candidates. The
three largest clusters give 78, 66 and 36 pairs.

A second fallback was pre-registered for this run and **not used**: the F&O universe has no
verifiable sector map on this host, and a single "Unclassified" group would have made the
candidate set all C(200,2) = 19,900 pairs — off-framework and ~22,000 cointegration tests.
The registered fallback is therefore KMeans on the same loading vectors at a fixed average
cluster size, deterministic given its seed. Inventing a 200-name sector map would have put
an unverifiable list at the centre of the multiple-testing denominator.

**Candidate pairs = 347.** That is *m*.

## 7. Capital: what the second sizing bought

Raising N from ₹10L to ₹25L per leg roughly doubles the pairs that can express their hedge
ratio — **65 → 135 of 347** — and changes nothing else, because no other gate depends on N.
It does not admit anything: the pairs it rescues fail BH and horizon regardless.

The median candidate first clears the 10 % β-accuracy bar at **N = ₹22.0 lakh per leg**,
which is almost exactly iteration 1's ₹22.5 lakh over Nifty 50 large caps. The **hypothesis**
this supports — it does not establish it — is that the lot floor is set by NSE's
per-contract value band rather than by universe choice: a mid-cap's larger lot count offsets
its lower price, so the hedge ratio a small book can express does not improve with breadth.
Two things keep it a hypothesis. The universes **overlap** — the Nifty 50 names are a subset
of the F&O 200, so these are not two independent observations — and the median also depends
on the β and price distribution of whichever universe is drawn. No band figure is derived
here; deriving one from the exchange's contract-value rule and predicting ₹22L from it is
what would settle it. FRAMEWORK §0 puts pair margin at ~40–50 % of one-leg N, so a
pair sized at ₹22L consumes roughly ₹9–11 lakh of margin against a ₹44L gross position.

## 8. The σ gate is still passing for the wrong reason

All 347 candidates clear σ_spread ≥ 50 bps. The gate does no work, for the two reasons
iteration 1 recorded, both of which the wider universe makes worse rather than better:

1. **σ is measured over 247 sessions; the trade lasts ≤ 5.** Within a 5-session cap an OU
   spread with half-life *h* reverts `1 − 2^(−5/h)` of the way on average. For NMDC/SAIL
   (h = 5.0) that is 50 %: expected capture ≈ 263 bps rather than the 525 bps a 2σ round
   trip suggests, against 24.3 bps all-in cost — ~11× coverage, not 22×. For a pair at
   h = 25 it is 13 %, and the multiple collapses.
2. **A low-|β| "pair" is mostly a single-stock position.** **132 of 347** candidates have
   |β| < 0.5 and **54** have |β| < 0.2. As shares these are *better* than iteration 1's
   27/54 and 13/54 — 38.0 % against 50.0 %, and 15.6 % against 24.1 % — so the wider
   universe produced proportionally fewer near-single-stock pairs, not more. What grew is
   the absolute count of them. Their "spread σ" is largely one stock's own volatility, and their apparent cost
   coverage is an artefact of it.

Cost is scaled on the **realised, lot-quantised leg ratio**, not the fitted β, since whole
lots are what trade and a small fitted β whose leg B still rounds up to one lot pays for
that lot in full. Across the 347 candidates all-in cost spans **17.3 – 66.9 bps** of leg-A
notional.

A minimum-|β| gate remains the open design fix. The case for it is no stronger than in
iteration 1 — proportionally the problem eased slightly — but it now removes 132 candidates
rather than 27, so its effect on *m*, and hence on the BH bar every surviving pair is
judged against, is material.

## 10. Honesty section — what this screen does not establish

1. **This is a screen, not a backtest.** No trading rule ran, no P&L exists, no return is
   claimed or implied anywhere in this document.
2. **Signals are computed on closes; the framework's execution rule is a pre-15:10
   snapshot.** Fitting β, σ, half-life and z on closes while the live rule fires on a 15:10
   snapshot estimates one price object and trades another (FRAMEWORK §3). Any pair this
   screen ever admits must be re-estimated on the snapshot series first.
3. **Statistics are fitted on cash equity closes; the trade executes in single-stock
   futures.** Basis noise, roll effects and futures microstructure mean the futures spread's
   σ, β and half-life are not the cash spread's. This remains the largest gap.
4. **Corporate-action adjustment is not guaranteed by the source** (§2). One name was caught
   only because the API returned both an adjusted and an unadjusted line; a name returning
   only an unadjusted line is caught only by the crude 0.4-log-return flag, which fired on
   no name here. A split inside the window would corrupt that pair's β and p.
5. **MWPL ban-history and event-calendar screens are UNCHECKED.** This host has neither
   feed. Both are recorded per pair as `mwpl_ban_screen = "unchecked"` and
   `event_screen = "unchecked"` in `all_candidates.csv` rather than silently passed. **This
   matters more at 200 names than at 49:** the F&O universe's mid-caps are exactly the names
   that hit MWPL ban periods, and no pair here has been cleared of one.
6. **No futures-liquidity screen was applied.** FRAMEWORK Phase 1 calls for a volume/OI
   floor and a spread proxy on the futures legs before pairing. This screen used cash data
   only, with no liquidity filter at all — again a bigger omission at 200 names than at 49,
   where Nifty 50 membership was itself a crude liquidity floor. Several admitted-adjacent
   names here are thinly traded in futures, and their true all-in cost exceeds the 17–27 bps
   model.
7. **The universe list is now exchange-derived rather than remembered**, which removes
   iteration 1's largest data-provenance caveat: membership comes from the scrip master's
   `FUTSTK` rows, not from the author's knowledge.
8. **The regression direction is a pre-registered coin flip** (alphabetical). Testing both
   directions and keeping the better p would have doubled the trial count silently.
9. **Only 110 of 200 names produced candidates** — 90 were OPTICS noise. A different but
   equally defensible clustering yields a different *m* and different BH bars. The
   pre-registered degeneracy test and the pre-registered KMeans fallback constrain that
   choice; they do not eliminate it.
10. **Hurst on 247 observations is noisy.** It removed 90 pairs on its own here; at this
    sample size some of those are rejections for sampling noise. Nothing rests on it — it is
    not a binding gate.
11. **Cointegration p comes from `coint()`, not `adfuller()` on the residual**, so it
    accounts for β being estimated. The anti-conservative `adfuller` route would make more
    pairs appear significant; whether any would clear the BH bar was not computed and so is
    not claimed.
12. **BH's guarantee is over a data-selected family, and widening the universe made this
    worse, not better.** The 347 candidates were chosen by clustering co-movement *on the
    same window* the cointegration test then uses, so survivors' p-values are tilted
    downward and are not uniform under the null. BH at m = 347 is *applied to* the
    post-selection family — it does not control FDR over that family either, since the
    selection destroys uniformity under the null — and certainly not over the implicit search of all **C(200,2) = 19,900** pairs,
    a denominator 57× larger than the one used, against 22× in iteration 1. Judged against
    19,900 the rank-1 bar would be 5.0e-6 and NMDC/SAIL's p of 0.0024 is 480× over it. For
    this run the direction is favourable — an anti-conservative correction still rejected
    nothing — but on any future run that admits a pair, this is the first objection to
    answer. Legs are also shared heavily across the 347 tests — COFORGE appears in 12 pairs
    and NMDC in 8 — so BH's positive-dependence assumption is doing unexamined work.
13. **Two estimator biases run in the screen's favour, and are stated because they do.**
    OLS AR(1) reversion speeds are biased toward *finding* reversion in finite samples, and
    "minimum half-life 5.00" is a minimum over 347 noisy estimates — a wider search over
    noisier estimates finds a smaller minimum even with no real improvement. The true
    fastest reversion is likely slower than 5.00 sessions, so the negative result is if
    anything understated. This is the main reason not to read §4's improved horizon numbers
    as a discovery.
14. **Multiplicity across iterations is not accounted for.** BH is applied within a run.
    This run re-tests most of iteration 1's 54 pairs, inside the same formation window, as
    part of its 347 — and nothing logs a screen run as a trial the way FRAMEWORK Phase 4
    logs a backtest. Re-running a screen with a modified universe or estimator until
    something passes is a selection channel, and it compounds with item 12. Moot while
    every run rejects everything; it must be closed before any run admits a pair.
15. **Two thresholds are this screen's choices, not the framework's.** FRAMEWORK §0 gives
    the σ band as 50–60 bps and the screen gates at the permissive end, 50; and q = 0.10
    appears nowhere in FRAMEWORK.md — it is a `screen.py` constant. Both are moot here (all
    347 pass σ, and nothing approaches BH at any defensible q), but neither was presented
    as a choice until now.
16. **A negative result is not proof of absence.** It says: on 200 F&O cash-close series
    over this 247-session window, with these pre-registered gates, nothing qualifies. Faster
    reversion may exist intraday — FRAMEWORK §0 notes sub-day half-lives cannot be estimated
    from daily closes at all.

## 11. Did widening the universe change the conclusion?

**No — and the way it failed to is informative.**

| | Iteration 1 (49 names) | v2 (200 names) | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Admitted | 0 of 54 | 0 of 347, at both N | unchanged |
| Horizon gate | 0 pass; min 6.51 | **1 passes; min 5.00** | genuinely improved |
| BH gate | 0 pass; best 14× over bar | 0 pass; best **8×** over bar | improved, still fatal |
| Lot gate at ₹10L | 10 of 54 (18.5 %) | 65 of 347 (18.7 %) | unchanged |
| Median min N per leg | ₹22.5L | ₹22.0L | unchanged |

Iteration 1 left one explicit open question — whether faster reversion exists in the wider
F&O universe. **It does, marginally:** 36 pairs inside 10 sessions against 4, and a minimum
that reaches the bar. That is the horizon constraint responding to breadth roughly as hoped —
subject to honesty item 13, since a minimum taken over 347 noisy estimates runs smaller than
one taken over 54 with no real improvement behind it.
But BH tightens as *m* grows, and it tightens faster than the best p improves in absolute
terms, so the two effects nearly cancel: 4× the universe, 6.4× the candidates, and the
distance from admission shrinks from 14× to 8.3× on the binding statistic.

That near-cancellation is what a global null predicts, and the prediction does not depend on
universe size: the expected minimum of *m* uniform p-values is ≈ 1/(m+1) while the rank-1 BH
bar is q/m, so best-p ÷ bar sits near **1/q = 10× for any m**. Iteration 1's 14× and v2's
8.3× straddle that line — the best pair in each universe behaved about as the smallest of
*m* null draws would. That is an argument that **breadth alone does not move the ratio**,
not a measured rate to extrapolate, and it cannot establish that no wider universe contains
genuine cointegration. What it does say is that adding names is not the lever; the remaining
levers are the ones FRAMEWORK already names, minute bars and futures series.

The lot floor holding near-constant across two overlapping universes is the cleanest new
observation, and §7 states what keeps it an observation rather than a law. On this evidence
a small book's pair-trading requirement is ~₹22 lakh per leg, and there is a mechanism that
would explain why breadth does not lower it.
