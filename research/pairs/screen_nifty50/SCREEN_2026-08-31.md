# Pair screen — Nifty 50, run 2026-08-31 (as-of close 2026-08-28)

**Result: 0 pairs admitted out of 54 candidates. There are no entry candidates and the
watchlist is empty.** The binding constraint is the horizon inequality: the fastest-
reverting candidate in the universe has an OU half-life of **6.5 sessions** against a
**≤ 5 session** bar, and no candidate survives multiple-testing correction either — the
smallest cointegration p is 0.027 against a rank-1 Benjamini–Hochberg bar of 0.0019 —
and no later rank clears its own bar either, so the full step-up rejects nothing.

This is a **screen**, not a backtest. It fits a formation window and evaluates admission
inequalities. It runs no trading rule, opens no position, and computes no return. Nothing
below is evidence that any pair is or would have been profitable.

## 2. Pre-filter

PCA on standardised daily log returns, 15 components; the first explains 31.2 % of
variance, the next four 7.2 / 5.7 / 3.9 / 3.3 %. Per-name loading vectors were clustered
with OPTICS.

The degeneracy test was **pre-registered in code before any data was seen**
(`screen.py`: fewer than 2 clusters, or a largest cluster over 60 % of names, or over
70 % noise → fall back to sector grouping). OPTICS returned 4 clusters, largest 16.3 % of
names, noise 55.1 % — **not degenerate, so the OPTICS branch ran** and the sector fallback
was not used. Cluster sizes are **8 / 5 / 5 / 4 names** (largest = 8/49 = 16.3 %), giving
28 / 10 / 10 / 6 pairs respectively. The remaining 27 names are OPTICS noise and
contribute no candidates.

**Candidate pairs = 54.** That is the multiple-testing denominator *m*; every
cointegration p-value below is judged against a Benjamini–Hochberg bar built on it.

## 6. Capital: the lot floor at N = ₹10 lakh

One futures lot is the finest granularity available, and at N = ₹10 lakh most Nifty 50
lots are already a large fraction of the position. 44 of 54 candidates miss the 10 %
β-accuracy bar for that reason alone.

M&M/MARUTI shows the mechanism: β = 0.730, but one lot of each is ₹6.67L and ₹6.69L, so
the nearest-lot portfolio realises β_eff = 1.003 — a 37.5 % distortion. The pair is not
statistically disqualified there; **the size is too small to express the hedge ratio.**

`min_notional_for_lot_gate` (in `all_candidates.csv`) reports where each pair would first
clear the gate: **median ₹22.5 lakh per leg**, i.e. more than double the mandated N.
FRAMEWORK §0 puts pair margin at ~40–50 % **of one-leg notional N** (17.7 % per leg, no
cross-underlying offset, plus an MTM buffer), so at N = ₹22.5L a single pair consumes
**₹9–11 lakh** of margin against a ₹45L two-leg gross position. This is a genuine finding about the interaction of lot sizes with
a small book, and it is independent of the statistical result.

## 7. The σ gate is passing for the wrong reason

Every candidate clears σ_spread ≥ 50 bps — the minimum is 306 bps, the median 593. The
gate is not doing any work, and two reasons deserve to be stated rather than left as an
apparently comfortable margin:

1. **σ is measured over 247 sessions; the trade lasts ≤ 5.** A 2σ entry captures 2σ only
   at full convergence. Within a 5-session cap, an OU spread with half-life *h* reverts
   `1 − 2^(−5/h)` of the way **on average** — individual paths can converge fully inside
   the cap, and the framework's exit-on-touch rule is there to take exactly those. For
   M&M/MARUTI (h = 6.5) the expected fraction is 41 %: 253 bps rather than 611 bps —
   ~9× the 27.0 bps all-in cost, not the 23× the raw ratio suggests. For a pair with
   h = 25 it is 13 %, and the coverage multiple collapses.
2. **A low-|β| "pair" is mostly a single-stock position, and its σ is mostly that stock's
   own volatility.** 27 of 54 candidates have |β| < 0.5 and 13 have |β| < 0.2.
   EICHERMOT/MARUTI at β = −0.047 is, economically, long EICHERMOT with a rounding error
   attached; its 582 bps "spread σ" is close to single-name dispersion. Its 47× apparent
   cost coverage is an artefact of that, not a margin of safety. A future revision should
   add a minimum-|β| gate or hedge on returns rather than log levels.

**Cost is scaled on the realised, lot-quantised leg ratio, not the fitted β.** Whole lots
are what trade: EICHERMOT/M&M has a fitted β of 0.021, but leg B still rounds up to one
full lot, so the realised ratio is 0.83 and the pair pays ~24.7 bps all-in rather than the
~12.4 bps its fitted β would imply. Scaling on the fitted β would have understated cost by
about half on exactly the low-β pairs whose quantisation error is largest. Across the 54
candidates all-in cost ranges 18.7–47.0 bps of leg-A notional.

mostly from pre-break continuous closes. That mismatch is small at this window length but
it is not zero, and it grows as the post-break sample grows.

## 9. Honesty section — what this screen does not establish

1. **This is a screen, not a backtest.** No trading rule ran, no P&L exists, no return is
   claimed or implied anywhere in this document.
2. **Signals are computed on closes; the framework's execution rule is a pre-15:10
   snapshot.** Fitting β, σ, half-life and z on the close series while the live rule fires
   on a 15:10 snapshot estimates one price object and trades another (FRAMEWORK §3). Any
   pair this screen ever admits must be re-estimated on the snapshot series before it
   becomes a template.
3. **Statistics are fitted on cash equity closes; the trade executes in single-stock
   futures.** Basis noise, roll effects and futures-specific microstructure mean the
   futures spread's σ, β and half-life are not the cash spread's. This is a distinct gap
   from (2) and arguably the larger one.
4. **The 2026-08-03 CAS break sits inside the formation window** — measured in §8, not
   eliminated. The 2026-04-01 STT break is also inside it; the cost model here uses the
   post-hike 12.3 bps hard / 17–27 bps all-in figures throughout, which is correct for
   today's trading and wrong for any pre-April sample.
5. **MWPL ban-history and event-calendar screens are UNCHECKED.** This host has neither
   feed. Both are recorded per pair as `mwpl_ban_screen = "unchecked"` and
   `event_screen = "unchecked"` in `all_candidates.csv` rather than silently passed. No
   pair here has been cleared of a recent ban period or an upcoming result date.
6. **No futures-liquidity screen was applied.** FRAMEWORK Phase 1 calls for a volume/OI
   floor and a spread proxy on the futures legs before pairing; this screen used cash data
   only and imposed no liquidity filter beyond Nifty 50 membership.
7. **The universe list is unverified** — see the header of `nifty50_members.py`. A wrong
   membership changes the candidate count and hence the BH denominator; it does not bias
   any individual pair's statistics.
8. **The regression direction is a pre-registered coin flip** (alphabetical). Testing both
   directions and keeping the better p would have doubled the trial count silently.
9. **Only 22 of 49 names produced candidates** — 27 were OPTICS noise. A different but
   equally defensible clustering would produce a different m and different BH bars. The
   pre-registered degeneracy test constrains that choice but does not eliminate it.
10. **Hurst on 247 observations is noisy.** It removed 14 pairs on its own; at this sample
    size its standard error is wide enough that some of those are rejections for sampling
    noise. The framework calls it belt-and-braces, and nothing here rests on it — it is
    not the binding gate.
11. **Cointegration p comes from `coint()`, not `adfuller()` on the residual**, so it
    accounts for β being estimated. The anti-conservative `adfuller` route would have made
    more pairs appear significant; whether any would then have cleared the BH bar was not
    computed, so it is not claimed — a rough translation of the best pair's p = 0.027 lands
    near 0.005–0.01, still above the 0.0019 rank-1 bar, but that is an estimate, not a run.
13. **BH's guarantee here is over a data-selected family.** The 54 candidates were chosen
    by clustering co-movement *on the same window* the cointegration test then uses, so the
    survivors' p-values are tilted downward and are not uniform under the null. BH at
    m = 54 therefore controls FDR over the post-selection family, not over the implicit
    search of all C(49,2) = 1,176 pairs. For this run the direction is favourable — an
    anti-conservative correction still rejected nothing — but on any future run that admits
    a pair, this is the first objection to answer. Relatedly, the 54 tests share legs
    heavily (M&M appears in 7), so BH's positive-dependence assumption is doing unexamined
    work; moot at zero rejections, material at one.
14. **Two estimator biases run in the screen's favour, and are stated because they do.**
    OLS AR(1) reversion speeds are biased toward *finding* reversion in finite samples,
    and "minimum half-life 6.5" is a minimum over 54 noisy estimates, biased downward
    again. The true fastest reversion in this universe is therefore likely *slower* than
    6.5 sessions — the negative result is if anything understated.
15. **A name missing any single session is dropped from the panel entirely** by
    `load_panel`, which is stricter than the fetch's own < 500-row rule. No name was
    dropped this way in this run; the dropped list is reported in `meta.json` so a future
    shrinking universe is visible rather than inferred.
12. **A negative result is not proof of absence.** It says: on 49 Nifty 50 cash-close
    series over this 247-session window, with these pre-registered gates, nothing
    qualifies. Faster reversion may exist intraday — FRAMEWORK §0 notes sub-day half-lives
    cannot be estimated from daily closes at all — and in the wider ~208-name F&O universe,
    which this screen did not touch.

## 11. What would change the answer

Stated as pre-registered next steps, not as a plan to relax gates until something passes:

1. **Minute bars.** The horizon gate is the binding one, and daily closes cannot see
   sub-day reversion. FRAMEWORK Phase 0's minute capture is the prerequisite for selecting
   pairs *for* the intraday unwind the mandate now permits.
2. **The full ~208-name F&O universe.** 54 candidates from 49 names is a small search;
   the framework's own design targets ~208 names. A larger m raises the BH bar per pair
   but searches a far larger space.
3. **Futures series rather than cash closes**, removing gap (3) above.
4. **A minimum-|β| gate**, so that low-β near-single-stock positions stop entering the
   candidate set at all.
