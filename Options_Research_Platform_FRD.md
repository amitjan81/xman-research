## Contents
## 1. Introduction
### 1.1 Purpose
This document specifies the functional requirements for a proprietary research platform whose purpose is to discover, validate, version, deploy and continuously improve trading signals for options strategies on Indian markets (NSE/BSE index and stock derivatives). Validated signals drive strategies executed on the xman trading platform. The document is intended as the basis for architecture design, build/buy decisions and phased delivery planning.
### 1.2 Scope
In scope: market and derived data management; the interactive research environment; a feature and signal library; backtesting and statistical validation; capital, sizing and capacity research; forward (paper) testing; signal versioning and a controlled software development lifecycle (SDLC) from research to production; production performance measurement; the feedback loop that drives signal improvement; and AI/LLM assistance across the research workflow. Out of scope for this document: order management and broker-layer risk enforcement, and execution logic inside xman itself. Note that position sizing and capacity are in scope as research questions (Section 8), even though their runtime enforcement lives in xman. The webhook-based integration with xman is specified only at the interface level and is explicitly a low-priority, later-phase item.

**Amended at v0.3.** The allocator (§20) introduces **runtime scope this document previously excluded**: signal weighting, the conflict veto, and the regime exposure multiplier all shape what is emitted, not merely what is researched. The exclusion is lifted deliberately rather than by omission, because "decides what to trade and when" cannot be specified without saying where those decisions take effect. §21 draws that boundary control by control, on one principle: **anything protecting capital rather than shaping research output must remain effective when this platform is stopped, unreachable, or emitting incorrectly** — and therefore belongs in xman, not here.
#### 1.2.1 Explicit exclusions (Won't, this roadmap)
The following are deliberately excluded so that future scope creep has something to be measured against: full order-book / market-microstructure capture and signals; high-frequency (sub-second) signal generation; multi-user governance, entitlement and approval workflow (the platform assumes a single accountable operator; approval gates are self-attestations with tamper-evident records per NFR-08); and reinforcement-learning execution agents. Any of these entering scope requires a new revision of this document.
### 1.3 Background and design philosophy
The requirements below are synthesised from three bodies of knowledge. First, the academic literature on backtest overfitting and financial machine learning, principally the work of Bailey and López de Prado on the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, the Probability of Backtest Overfitting, purged and combinatorial purged cross-validation, and the "research factory" organisation of quantitative work. Second, publicly documented practice at systematic funds and multi-manager platforms, where the consistent lessons are that point-in-time data discipline and walk-forward validation must be pipeline requirements rather than researcher options, that a hybrid of interpretable factor signals plus machine-learning refinements is the dominant production architecture, and that signal decay is the central long-term challenge the platform must be designed to detect and manage. Third, the modern MLOps canon: feature stores to eliminate training/serving skew, model registries with full lineage, staged promotion (research → paper → production), drift monitoring and champion/challenger evaluation.
Three principles govern everything in this document. (1) Anti-overfitting controls are enforced by the platform, not left to researcher discipline: every backtest records how many variations were tried, and headline metrics are always reported alongside selection-bias-adjusted equivalents. (2) Research/production parity: the same signal code, feature definitions and cost model run in backtest, paper and live modes, so a signal cannot behave differently in production than in research for architectural reasons. (3) Everything is versioned and reproducible: a signal result is a deterministic function of (data snapshot, code version, parameter set), and any historical result can be regenerated exactly.
### 1.4 Requirement conventions and verification

**Every control names its enforcement point.** A requirement that creates a record must name the gate or job that reads it and the action taken when it reads badly. A record nothing acts on is documentation, not a control — this document has twice created one and had to be told. FR-ENF-01 states this for controls crossing the xman boundary; it applies to every control here, and §19's gate table is where most of them land.

**On the boundary with architecture.** Requirements state what must be true; how to make it true belongs in the HLD and in architecture decision records. Where this document names a *statistical method* — deflated Sharpe, probability of backtest overfitting, purged cross-validation — that is a requirement rather than an implementation: "correct for multiple testing" without naming the correction is unverifiable, and the control exists precisely because a plausible-sounding substitute would not do. Where earlier revisions named *tooling or topology* — a specific notebook product, a specific agent arrangement — those have been generalised, since they are choices rather than obligations.
Requirements are identified as FR-<MODULE>-<NN> and prioritised using MoSCoW. Following the review, MoSCoW labels are scoped to the Phase 1 timebox defined in Section 25 (Phasing) — "Must" means "Phase 1 is not credible without it", not "important in general" — which is the only condition under which MoSCoW forces genuine trade-offs. Section 24 defines a Minimal Credible Core (MCC), the strictly smaller subset sufficient to run one honest experiment end to end, which is the first thing to build.
Verification: each module preamble names the default verification method for its requirements using the standard categories (inspection, analysis, demonstration, test). Full per-requirement fit criteria in the scale/meter/threshold form are deliberately deferred to the phase specification of whichever phase a requirement enters — writing ~100 fit criteria before the Minimal Credible Core exists would be ceremony — but four requirements the review identified as untestable as written (FR-RES-02, FR-RES-06, FR-DATA-08, FR-MON-04) have been rewritten with measurable criteria in this revision. Detectors that can be over-aggressive (leakage detection, outlier IV detection) must ship with negative controls: known-clean inputs on which they must not fire.

## 2. System Overview
The platform is organised as a pipeline of **eleven** cooperating modules around a shared data backbone. Data flows left to right in the happy path — from raw market data through research, validation and deployment into production monitoring — and right to left in the feedback path, where production performance drives signal refinement and retirement.

Two of the eleven were previously specified in a separate document and are consolidated here at v0.3: the **Hypothesis Engine**, which decides what is worth researching at all, and the **Allocator**, which decides what runs live and at what size. They bracket the pipeline — one upstream of all research, one downstream of all validation — which is why specifying them apart from the rest was a mistake.

| Module | Role |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis Engine** | Registry of falsifiable claims with stated mechanisms; the seed catalogue (Appendix B); exploration intake; frequency scoping. Decides what enters research at all (§15–§17). |
| Data Layer | Point-in-time store of NSE/BSE options, futures and cash market data; derived analytics (IV, Greeks, surfaces); data quality control; regulatory-epoch registry. |
| Research Environment | Interactive workspace where hypotheses are formed and explored using the statistical toolbox and the feature library, with the exploration/trial boundary instrumented. |
| Signal & Feature Library | Versioned registry of features and signals as code, with metadata, provenance, lineage and reuse. |
| Backtesting Engine | Event-driven simulation of options strategies with a realistic Indian cost, margin and settlement model, epoch-annotated reporting and tail-aware metrics. |
| Validation Service | Statistical gatekeeper: walk-forward and combinatorial purged cross-validation, deflated performance metrics, overfitting probability, multiple-testing corrections — with thresholds locked before results are visible. |
| Capital, Sizing & Capacity | Sizing policy as a researched, validated artefact; portfolio risk budget; participation caps and per-signal capacity estimates. |
| Forward Test Harness | Paper trading of candidate signals on live data; compares realised behaviour to backtest expectations. |
| Deployment & SDLC | Promotion gates, environment management, release/rollback, audit trail from hypothesis to production. |
| Production Monitoring & Feedback | Live vs. expected attribution, decay and drift detection, pre-registered kill criteria, automated alerts, structured feedback into research. |
| **Regime & Allocator** | Regime detection (if FR-REG-01 justifies building it), the exposure multiplier as a precautionary risk control, and the allocator deciding which live signals run, at what margin weight, and what happens when two conflict (§18, §20). |

AI capabilities are a layer that assists inside several modules (idea generation in Research, code synthesis in the Signal Library, anomaly narration in Monitoring), always subject to the same validation gates as human-authored work, and additionally subject to parametric look-ahead controls (Section 13). The xman execution interface sits at the far right of the pipeline and consumes production signal triggers over webhooks.
## 3. Functional Requirements — Data Layer (FR-DATA)
Every documented failure mode of quantitative research platforms traces back to data: lookahead bias, survivorship bias, silently revised histories, and inconsistent datasets between research and production. The data layer therefore carries the strictest requirements in the platform. For options research specifically, the raw options chain is not enough; the platform must persist derived analytics (implied volatility, Greeks, fitted surfaces) as first-class, point-in-time historical data. Two properties added in this revision deserve emphasis: expired option contracts generally fall out of vendor instrument masters and cannot be re-requested afterwards, making prospective capture urgent from day one and impossible to satisfy in arrears (to be confirmed empirically with the chosen vendor, but the asymmetry of consequences favours acting as though it is true); and the 2024–2026 Indian regulatory changes mean the market's structure is not stationary across the backtest history, so regulatory epochs are first-class data. Default verification method for this module: test (automated checks against known fixtures), with inspection for catalogue/registry requirements.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-DATA-01 | Ingest and persist NSE/BSE derivatives data (options chains and futures for index and stock underlyings) at minimum at 1-minute bar resolution, with end-of-day bhavcopy as the reconciliation baseline. (Tick-data extensibility is recorded as assumption C5, not as a schema requirement.) | Must |
| FR-DATA-02 | Persist underlying cash/index data corporate-action adjusted, retaining both raw and adjusted series, with the adjustment methodology versioned. | Must |
| FR-DATA-03 | All data is stored point-in-time: every record carries both event time and knowledge time (when it became available), and all research APIs default to as-of queries that make lookahead access impossible rather than merely discouraged. | Must |
| FR-DATA-04 | Persist contract reference data as history: lot sizes, tick sizes, strike schemes, expiry calendars, ban lists, margin parameters and regulatory changes, each valid over a date range, so backtests use the rules that applied on the simulated date. | Must |
| FR-DATA-05 | Compute and persist implied volatility (using a robust solver of production quality, e.g. the Jäckel "Let's Be Rational" approach) and first-order Greeks (delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho) for every option record, as point-in-time history. Second-order Greeks (vanna, charm, volga) are computed on demand for strategies that need them. | Must (2nd-order: Could) |
| FR-DATA-06 | Fit and persist arbitrage-free implied volatility surfaces per underlying using a parametric model (SVI/SSVI as baseline, SABR as an alternative), storing fitted parameters, fit quality metrics and flagged arbitrage violations. Fitting frequency is a configuration (end-of-day baseline; intraday where a strategy requires it) rather than mandated per-minute. | Should |
| FR-DATA-07 | Compute and persist standard volatility analytics: realised volatility over multiple windows and estimators, variance risk premium (IV minus RV), IV rank/percentile, term-structure state (contango/backwardation) and skew metrics, as reusable point-in-time series. | Must |
| FR-DATA-08 | Run automated data-quality control on ingest: schema validation, gap detection, stale-price detection, outlier IV detection (default rule: a smile point more than a configurable threshold — initial value 20 vol points — from the interpolation of its strike neighbours at the same expiry is flagged), cross-source reconciliation, and quarantine of suspect records with alerting. QC status of every record is queryable, and the outlier detector ships with a negative control set of known-clean chains on which it must not fire. | Must |
| FR-DATA-09 | Support immutable, named data snapshots. A backtest or validation run always executes against a snapshot identifier, and re-running against the same snapshot reproduces byte-identical inputs. | Must |
| FR-DATA-10 | Late corrections and vendor restatements are applied as new versions, never as in-place mutation; the platform can answer "what did we believe on date D" for any series. | Must |
| FR-DATA-11 | Maintain a data catalogue describing every dataset, its source, update cadence, known limitations and QC history. | Should |
| FR-DATA-12 | Ingest supplementary datasets useful for options signals: India VIX, FII/DII flows, open-interest and participant-wise OI data, event calendars (expiries, results, RBI/Fed meetings, budget) as point-in-time series. | Should |
| FR-DATA-13 | Support alternative/unstructured data (news, filings, transcripts) with embeddings aligned to market timestamps for research use. | Could |
| FR-DATA-14 | Prospective capture: continuous daily capture of the full live derivatives universe (all listed contracts, including those expiring) begins at platform day one and is monitored as a production job, because expired contracts generally cannot be backfilled from vendors afterwards. A backfill-depth target for pre-platform history is recorded against constraint C4. | Must |
| FR-DATA-15 | Persist daily instrument-master snapshots (the full list of listed contracts and their attributes each day) as point-in-time history, so the tradable universe on any historical date is answerable without reconstruction. | Must |
| FR-DATA-16 | Maintain a regulatory-epoch registry: named epochs with effective dates for structural market changes (e.g. the November 2024 contract-size and weekly-expiry changes, the October 2024 STT change, the 2025 expiry-day migration between exchanges), each with a description of what analytical assumptions it breaks. Epoch boundary dates are verified against primary exchange/SEBI circulars before entry. | Must |
| FR-DATA-17 | Persist index constituent history with effective dates and retain delisted symbols and their full history, so a universe resolver can answer "what was in the index on date D" and survivorship bias cannot re-enter through the universe definition. | Should |
| FR-DATA-18 | Where the vendor supplies implied volatility and Greeks, reconcile vendor-supplied against independently computed values and persist the residual as a data-quality series; alert on drift in the residual. This catches vendor error and own-model error simultaneously. | Should |

## 4. Functional Requirements — Research Environment (FR-RES)
The research environment is where hypotheses are formed and killed. Institutional practice separates exploratory work (fast, interactive, permissive) from validated claims (slow, gated, recorded) — but the boundary between the two is where trial-count integrity is won or lost, and this revision takes an explicit position on it (FR-RES-09): the platform instruments what it can, requires declared exploration sessions for the rest, and treats the recorded trial count as a lower bound whose consequence is stated on every deflated statistic. Default verification method for this module: demonstration, with test for the SDK logging behaviour.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-RES-01 | Provide an interactive notebook-based research workspace with pre-configured access to the data layer, feature library, backtester and validation service through a single research SDK. | Must |
| FR-RES-02 | Provide a curated statistical toolbox as a maintained library: stationarity and unit-root tests, GARCH-family volatility models, regime detection (HMM, Markov-switching), Hurst exponent, Kalman filtering, cointegration, wavelet decomposition, and bootstrap/Monte Carlo resampling. Fit criterion: each tool has at least one executable example notebook running against a pinned platform snapshot, and the example suite runs green in CI. | Must |
| FR-RES-03 | Every research project begins with a hypothesis record in a structured registry: economic rationale, predicted effect, target instruments, evaluation plan and status (open, supported, rejected, promoted). The registry is searchable and hypotheses are linked to all experiments run against them. | Must |
| FR-RES-04 | All experiment runs through the SDK (parameters, data snapshot, code version, results) are automatically logged to an experiment tracker without researcher action; nothing run through the pipeline can be unrecorded. | Must |
| FR-RES-05 | Research workloads are resource-partitioned from live trading (CPU/memory/IO quotas and priority, per constraint C2): live trading must be protected from research load. Where hardware is shared this is a partitioning guarantee, not an isolation guarantee, and NFR-09 states the enforcement mechanism. | Must |
| FR-RES-06 | Provide options-specific exploratory tooling, minimally: (a) payoff and Greek-profile visualisation for arbitrary multi-leg structures; (b) surface visualisation over time; (c) scenario analysis under joint spot/vol/time shocks. Fit criterion: a researcher can render each of (a)–(c) for any historical date from the SDK in a single call. | Should |
| FR-RES-07 | Provide reusable research report templates so that a completed study (hypothesis, method, results, deflated metrics, decision) renders to a standard document for review. | Should |
| FR-RES-08 | Support scheduled research jobs (e.g. nightly re-estimation of a regime model) with results published back to the data layer as derived series. | Should |
| FR-RES-09 | Exploration-to-trial boundary: (a) every SDK evaluation of a signal or parameterised variant is logged as a trial automatically, including inside notebooks; (b) ad-hoc exploration that bypasses the SDK is permitted only inside a declared exploration session, opened against a hypothesis id, whose existence and duration are recorded; (c) the platform treats the recorded trial count as a lower bound, and every DSR/PBO report carries the annotation that it is computed from a lower-bound count and is therefore an optimistic bound. Undeclared exploration is a process violation surfaced by periodic self-audit against notebook execution history. | Must |

## 5. Functional Requirements — Signal & Feature Library (FR-SIG)
A signal is executable code with a contract, not a notebook cell. The feature store pattern is adopted for its central benefit: a feature is defined once, computed identically for backtest and live use, and reused across signals — eliminating training/serving skew. Signals compose features into a trigger with defined semantics that xman-executed strategies can consume. Default verification method: test.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-SIG-01 | Provide a feature store in which every feature is defined as versioned code with a declared schema, update frequency and data dependencies; features are computed once and served consistently to research (offline, historical) and production (online, latest) consumers. | Must |
| FR-SIG-02 | Define a standard signal contract: a signal declares its universe, features consumed, output semantics (e.g. entry/exit/target-position, direction, strength, confidence), evaluation frequency, and its intended strategy mapping. | Must |
| FR-SIG-03 | Signals and features are stored in git-backed repositories; a signal version is a commit, and the registry records for each version its lineage: hypothesis id, experiments, data snapshots, validation results and review decisions. | Must |
| FR-SIG-04 | The same signal artefact runs unmodified in three modes — backtest, paper and live — with mode-specific data adapters. **Same code is necessary but not sufficient for parity.** The requirement is a **deterministic decision function producing identical decisions under an identical event stream**: identical code can still decide differently through event ordering, late or missing data, warm-up state, feature-cache state, retries, numerical precision, corporate-action processing or session/timezone handling. Parity is therefore defined over `data → timestamp → feature → signal → decision`, not over the artefact alone. | Must |
| FR-SIG-09 | **Replay verification.** Re-running the recorded live event stream (§11's live-observed dataset) through the research path shall reproduce the live decisions exactly. This is what converts FR-SIG-04 from an architectural claim into a tested property, and it is the only way ordering- and state-derived divergence becomes visible at all. | Must |
| FR-SIG-05 | Support signal composition: ensembles or meta-signals built from registered signals, with lineage preserved through composition. | Should |
| FR-SIG-06 | Track cross-signal correlation and feature overlap in the registry so that crowding/redundancy among signals is visible before capital is allocated to a new one. | Should |
| FR-SIG-07 | Support deprecation workflow: a signal version can be marked deprecated with a reason and a successor, and the registry prevents deprecated versions from being promoted. | Should |
| FR-SIG-08 | Record signal provenance (original idea, literature-derived, AI-generated, hybrid) on every signal. **The haircut applies to sizing, not to the gate.** Literature-derived signals carry a default discount on *expected effect size used in position sizing*, not a transformation of backtest P&L at gate evaluation. Haircutting P&L distorts the statistic being tested; discounting a publicly known effect when deciding how much to risk is exactly right. *The correct abstraction is a prior on replication probability rather than a fixed multiplier; that requires a Bayesian evaluation frame the platform does not have, so this is a recorded interim crudeness with the prior-based version as the target.* The discount reflects the documented post-publication decay of published predictors (the canonical figures — roughly 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication — are equity cross-section results; their application to options strategies is an extrapolation and is labelled as such, with the haircut a reviewable configuration rather than a constant). | Should |

## 6. Functional Requirements — Backtesting Engine (FR-BT)
For Indian options the difference between a naive and an honest backtest is frequently the difference between an apparent edge and none. The engine must model the full Indian cost stack point-in-time, realistic fills, European cash settlement, and margin so that returns are computed on realistic capital. This revision resolves the cost-model position explicitly against constraint C3 rather than hedging it, adds regulatory-epoch annotation so results are never silently pooled across structural breaks, and adds cost-breakeven and tail-aware headline metrics. Default verification method: test against golden fixture strategies with hand-computed costs and settlements.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-BT-01 | Provide an event-driven backtesting engine for multi-leg options strategies driven by registered signals, supporting intraday and positional (multi-day) holding, partial fills, and expiry handling. | Must |
| FR-BT-02 | Model the full Indian transaction cost stack as point-in-time configuration: STT (sell-side on premium; intrinsic-value STT on exercised ITM options), brokerage, exchange charges, SEBI turnover fees, GST and stamp duty, each with effective-date history so multi-year backtests apply the rates in force at the time. | Must |
| FR-BT-03 | (a) If historical bid/ask quote data is available (constraint C3), model slippage as a configurable fraction of the observed spread, scaled by number of legs, order size relative to volume, and volatility regime; never default to mid-price fills. (b) If quote data is not available, the cost model is learned from realised fills, and the platform must implement and document its known limitations: a stated prior for never-traded strategies (cold start and circularity); recognition that fills-only calibration cannot see the opportunity cost of unfilled orders; a reference-price policy acknowledging stale last-trades at illiquid strikes; minimum-sample floors per (moneyness × time-of-day × size) cell with shrinkage toward the prior below the floor; and a bias statement that fills embed the adverse selection and regimes of already-traded behaviour. Position (b) carries a revisit clause: if quote data later becomes available, (a) supersedes and the fills-based model becomes a validation input. | Must |
| FR-BT-15 | **Cost is an uncertainty envelope, not a point estimate.** The cost model emits a central estimate *and* a credible interval derived from its own calibration — the shrinkage and sample-floor machinery in FR-BT-03(b) already produces the dispersion needed. **Promotion must survive the upper credible bound, not an arbitrary multiplier.** A fixed 2× or 3× stress is a guess about uncertainty the model can measure directly; where the calibration is thin the interval widens on its own, which is the correct behaviour and exactly where arbitrary multipliers mislead. **Under C3 branch (a)** — quotes available, spread-fraction model — the interval derives instead from observed spread dispersion across the matching (moneyness × time-of-day × size) cells; the requirement is unconditional and both branches therefore define its mechanism. | Must |
| FR-BT-16 | **Execution feasibility is a distinct output from execution cost.** "What would this have cost?" and "could this have been filled at all?" are different questions and FR-BT-02/03 answer only the first. Each simulated entry and exit records a feasibility verdict from traded volume and open interest at those strikes in that window — did the contract trade at all, was there sufficient volume, and for multi-leg structures **could every leg have been filled**, with unfilled-leg risk reported rather than assumed away. Feasibility failures are a first-class result, not a cost adjustment. *Scope note: queue position, cancel/replace behaviour and crossed-market handling are excluded — they require order-book data the platform will not have (§1.2.1, C3) and are out of scope at mid-frequency.* | Must |
| FR-BT-04 | Model expiry correctly for NSE/BSE index options: European exercise, cash settlement against the official closing value, OTM legs expiring worthless; model physical settlement rules for stock derivatives where applicable. | Must |
| FR-BT-05 | Model margin: SPAN/exposure or the exchange-prescribed methodology in force, peak margin rules, expiry-day additional margin on short options where in force, and margin benefit for hedged structures, so capital usage and returns-on-capital are realistic; simulate margin calls on adverse moves. | Must |
| FR-BT-06 | Every run records a **run manifest**, and is exactly reproducible from it. Three identifiers are not enough: the manifest is **data snapshot + code commit + dependency lockfile hash + container image digest + model and feature versions + parameter config + cost-config version + random seeds + runtime config**. Numerical results depend on the dependency set, BLAS implementation and thread count as much as on the code, so a tuple that omits them promises a reproducibility the design cannot deliver. | Must |
| FR-BT-07 | Report a standard metric set per run: net and gross CAGR, Sharpe (and probabilistic/deflated Sharpe per Section 7), Sortino, max drawdown and duration, win rate, profit factor, turnover, cost drag, margin utilisation, per-leg fill diagnostics, and **efficiency denominators — return per unit of margin consumed, per unit of liquidity consumed (participation against traded volume and open interest), and per unit of tail risk**; equity curve and full trade log are persisted. A strategy with a higher Sharpe that consumes far more capital and liquidity may be the worse addition to a book, and a metric set without those denominators cannot show it. | Must |
| FR-BT-08 | Support cost/slippage stress: re-evaluate a stored run under perturbed cost and slippage assumptions without re-simulation where possible, to expose fragile edges. | Should |
| FR-BT-09 | Support parameter sweeps and batch runs on research compute, with all variants logged to the experiment tracker (feeding the trial count used by overfitting statistics). | Must |
| FR-BT-10 | Support regime-sliced and event-sliced reporting (by volatility regime, trend regime, expiry week, event days) as first-class diagnostics. | Should |
| FR-BT-11 | Support backtesting on synthetic/resampled data (block bootstrap, Monte Carlo paths from fitted models) as an additional robustness check. | Could |
| FR-BT-12 | Every backtest result is annotated with the regulatory epochs (FR-DATA-16) its window spans, per-epoch sub-results are reported wherever a window crosses an epoch boundary, and pooling across epochs without a recorded justification on the run is barred by default. Day-of-week, expiry-day and DTE-based features are automatically flagged when their window crosses an epoch that changed expiry structure. | Must |
| FR-BT-13 | Report the cost-breakeven multiple as a headline metric: the multiplier on modelled total costs at which the strategy's net expectancy reaches zero. Strategies below a configurable multiple (initial value 2×) are flagged as cost-fragile at gate evaluation. | Must |
| FR-BT-14 | For negatively-skewed strategies (short-premium structures in particular), headline reporting must include tail-aware metrics — CVaR/expected shortfall at configurable confidence, worst-N-day P&L, and skewness/kurtosis of returns — so that Sharpe-type ratios cannot be the sole basis of a promotion decision. | Must |

## 7. Functional Requirements — Validation & Anti-Overfitting (FR-VAL)
This module is the platform's scientific conscience: high simulated performance is easily achieved after trying relatively few configurations, and overfitted strategies systematically underperform out of sample. Walk-forward analysis is the baseline; combinatorial purged cross-validation prevents leakage from overlapping labels and serial correlation; the Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects the headline Sharpe for trials, non-normality and selection bias; and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting quantifies how likely the selected configuration is a fluke. This revision adds three controls the review correctly demanded: gate thresholds are locked before results are visible (the discipline FR-FWD-03 already applied to forward tests, now applied to the statistical gates themselves); multiple-testing corrections across a search family; and computable minimum sample lengths. On significance hurdles, the literature is genuinely unsettled — one influential line argues newly discovered effects should clear a far higher bar than convention, and a substantial rebuttal argues such hurdles are over-restrictive — so the platform ships both stances as named presets and forces the choice before results are visible rather than embedding one silently. Default verification method: test against synthetic strategies with known properties (a known-overfit generator the gates must reject, a known-genuine generator they must pass).

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-VAL-01 | Provide walk-forward validation as a standard service: rolling train/test windows with re-optimisation, aggregate out-of-sample statistics, and parameter-stability diagnostics across windows. | Must |
| FR-VAL-02 | Provide purged k-fold and combinatorial purged cross-validation with configurable purge and embargo periods, generating multiple backtest paths and reporting the distribution of out-of-sample performance rather than a single number. | Must |
| FR-VAL-03 | Compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio for every candidate signal, using the platform-recorded trial count (all logged experiment variants for the hypothesis, per FR-RES-09) as the number of trials — the researcher cannot self-report a smaller number. Every DSR report carries the lower-bound annotation defined in FR-RES-09(c). | Must |
| FR-VAL-04 | Compute the Probability of Backtest Overfitting for every promotion candidate and enforce a configurable maximum as a hard promotion gate. | Must |
| FR-VAL-05 | Enforce validation as pipeline gates: a signal cannot enter forward testing without passing the configured walk-forward/CPCV, DSR and PBO thresholds, and the gate configuration itself is versioned. | Must |
| FR-VAL-06 | Support dependence-aware statistical comparison of strategies (e.g. bootstrap tests for Sharpe differences) so that "improvement" claims between signal versions are statistically grounded. | Should |
| FR-VAL-07 | Provide sensitivity/ablation tooling: performance by feature ablation, by parameter perturbation, by subsample, and transaction-cost sensitivity, rendered into the standard research report. | Should |
| FR-VAL-08 | Detect and warn on common leakage patterns automatically (features referencing future timestamps, labels overlapping test windows, use of revised rather than point-in-time data). The detector ships with a negative control suite of known-clean pipelines on which it must not fire. | Should |
| FR-VAL-09 | Gate thresholds are locked per hypothesis before any gated result is visible: the threshold set (DSR minimum, PBO maximum, hurdle preset, multiple-testing haircut per FR-VAL-10 — **not** the FR-SIG-08 literature discount, which is a sizing parameter and is locked separately under FR-DEC-06) is recorded against the hypothesis id at evaluation-plan time, and changing thresholds after results exist requires opening a new, linked hypothesis record — the old record and its outcome remain. Hurdle presets ship in at least two named stances (conservative high-hurdle; FDR-bounded conventional) and the preset choice is part of the locked configuration. | Must |
| FR-VAL-10 | Apply multiple-testing correction across a declared search family: when a hypothesis sweeps configurations, report family-corrected significance (Bonferroni/Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg–Yekutieli variants) and a haircut Sharpe alongside per-configuration statistics. | Must |
| FR-VAL-12 | **A sealed temporal holdout is a first-class architectural object, and the platform's strongest control.** Every statistical control in this section — walk-forward, CPCV, DSR, PBO, minimum length, multiple-testing correction — assumes honest accounting of what was tried. A sealed holdout does not. It is the defence that survives an adaptive research process (FR-EXP-08) precisely because it depends on no accounting at all. | Must |
| FR-VAL-13 | The holdout is **inaccessible by construction, not by convention**: unreadable to the research slice, to notebooks, to optimisation and feature-selection jobs, to LLM prompts and to any agent. Enforcement is an access boundary, not a policy — the platform contains autonomous agents with lake access, and a rule they are asked to respect is not a control. | Must |
| FR-VAL-14 | The holdout is **evaluated once per candidate by the `promotion` principal**, under a protocol declared before evaluation, at the gate named in §19. Repeated evaluation converts it back into a training set, one look at a time. | Must |
| FR-VAL-15 | **"Candidate" is defined as a signal version hash.** Sibling variants are separate candidates and each consumes a look — which is why they are governed by a **family submission budget** (FR-VAL-16) rather than treated as free. Without this definition, "evaluated once per candidate" is defeated by submitting ten near-identical siblings, taking ten verdicts, and conditioning the eleventh on them: the adaptive selection of FR-EXP-08, running on the holdout itself. | Must |
| FR-VAL-16 | **A locked family submission budget** caps total holdout looks per mechanism family per period, and an **aggregate multiplicity correction across looks** is applied and reported — the looks are a search, and a search that is not corrected is not controlled. Budget and correction are locked with the FR-VAL-09 threshold set. | Must |
| FR-VAL-17 | **Verdicts are withheld from the generating process** until the family's budget window closes. A verdict returned to a campaign still generating candidates is a training signal, and FR-AI-03's information boundaries are extended to cover holdout verdicts explicitly. | Must |
| FR-VAL-18 | **Construction is specified, not left to the implementer.** The holdout is (a) **temporally contiguous and most-recent**, (b) **epoch-aware** — it does not straddle a boundary from FR-DATA-16, since a window spanning a structural break tests two markets, (c) of **minimum segment length satisfying FR-VAL-11's MinBTL logic for the holding periods in scope** — a segment too short to support significance for a multi-day positional strategy validates nothing — and (d) **replenished from prospective capture**, with newly captured data accruing sealed by default. Consumed segments are **not** released to research; releasing them makes the seal a delay. | Must |
| FR-VAL-19 | **The holdout must postdate the training cutoff of every model in the candidate's lineage.** A historical window preceding an LLM's cutoff is *not unseen* by a candidate that model generated — the weights contain the period, which is FR-AI-08's parametric look-ahead applied to the holdout. A candidate whose lineage includes a model whose cutoff postdates the holdout is **ineligible for holdout evaluation**. This makes the holdout necessarily prospective for machine-generated candidates, and is a second reason prospective capture (FR-DATA-14) is on the critical path. | Must |
| FR-VAL-20 | **Failed-evaluation semantics are defined in advance.** An evaluation that fails for a platform reason — crash, infrastructure fault, or a data-quality defect later found in the holdout zone — does **not** consume the look, and the reason is recorded. An evaluation that completes consumes the look **whatever the verdict**. Leaving this to be decided afterwards recreates precisely the post-hoc discretion FR-VAL-09 exists to remove. | Must |
| FR-VAL-21 | **Holdout capacity is computed and reported before the first evaluation**, not discovered by exhaustion: total sealed span, segment length, looks available, expected candidate flow, and the resulting horizon at which the resource is depleted. This document required arithmetic of the two-tier protocol and owes it here — a depleting control whose budget is unknown is a control with an unknown expiry date. | Must |
| FR-VAL-11 | Compute and enforce the minimum backtest length implied by the recorded trial count (and correspondingly the minimum track-record length for live evaluation): a candidate whose backtest window is shorter than the computed minimum for its trial count cannot pass the gate, because an apparently significant result is then expected by chance. | Must |

## 11. Functional Requirements — Production Monitoring & Performance (FR-MON)
Signal decay is the norm: genuine edges attract competition and fade. Monitoring makes decay visible early and distinguishes the two failure classes that must never be confused — technical failure (the pipeline computes something different from research: a bug) and statistical failure (correct implementation, decayed prediction). Because realised P&L is a slow and noisy label, monitoring leads with label-free indicators. This revision adds pre-registered kill criteria: the retirement threshold is recorded at promotion time, before decay makes the decision uncomfortable. Default verification method: test with injected drift/divergence fixtures.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-MON-01 | Track live performance per signal and per strategy: P&L, hit rate, slippage vs. model, turnover, drawdown — attributed to signal version, and always displayed against the backtest/forward-test expectation bands for the same version. | Must |
| FR-MON-02 | Monitor feature drift: distribution of every production feature versus its research baseline (e.g. PSI/K-S), with alert thresholds; monitor prediction/trigger-frequency drift the same way. | Must |
| FR-MON-03 | Detect research/production divergence: periodically recompute production feature values through the research path on the same inputs and alert on disagreement (training/serving skew detection). | Must |
| FR-MON-04 | Implement decay diagnostics: rolling out-of-sample statistics with change-point detection, and a triage flow with the following minimum structure: every incident is classified technical vs. statistical within a defined SLA, each class has a written playbook (technical: reproduce, bisect, fix, verify parity; statistical: de-risk per FR-MON-05, review against kill criteria, route to research), and every incident record links its classification evidence. | FR-MON-05 | Automated de-risking rules: configurable conditions (drawdown, drift severity, divergence) under which a signal is automatically demoted from production to paper mode pending review. | Must |

## 13. Functional Requirements — AI/LLM Integration (FR-AI)
The 2025–26 research literature (AlphaAgent at KDD '25; RD-Agent(Q); QuantaAlpha; XALPHA; FactorMiner at KDD '26; and related work) converges on a clear pattern: LLM agents materially accelerate hypothesis generation, code implementation and diagnostic work, but fail in predictable ways — overfitting via unlogged search, reward hacking against the backtest metric, and confident claims without evidence. The mitigations are architectural: multi-role agent designs communicating through the structured registry, evidence-bound claims, and regularisation of generated factors for originality and complexity. This revision adds the failure mode the review correctly identified as missing: parametric look-ahead. A model trained after the period being backtested already knows what happened; published work shows backtested LLM-agent performance collapsing once evaluation moves past the model's knowledge window, and the bias is invisible — it produces no error, only a better-looking result. AI-generated signals traverse the same FR-VAL and FR-DEP gates as human-authored ones — the platform grants them no shortcut.
An open problem is recorded rather than implied settled: no published method yet reconciles machine-scale hypothesis search with Deflated-Sharpe/PBO-style corrections — the corrections assume a countable, honest trial family, and agentic search strains both properties. FR-AI-06's position (log every candidate as a trial) is deliberately conservative and is a stance taken in the absence of established practice; it should be revisited as the literature matures.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-AI-01 | Integrate LLM assistance into the research environment for hypothesis generation and literature synthesis, with generated hypotheses written into the hypothesis registry in the standard falsifiable schema (economic claim, predictors, sample, null). | Should |
| FR-AI-02 | LLM code synthesis: translate a registered hypothesis into candidate feature/signal code conforming to the platform contract, executed only through the standard experiment pipeline (auto-logged, snapshot-pinned). | Should |
| FR-AI-03 | Adopt a multi-role agent design with separate contexts communicating via the registry rather than a single self-evaluating loop, and with **declared information boundaries enforced by the platform**. The boundaries carry the weight, not the role count: an implementer that cannot see out-of-sample results cannot tune toward them; a critic that receives hypothesis and methodology *without the result* attacks the mechanism rather than rationalising the number; a promotion judge that receives only the immutable evidence package cannot be influenced by the narrative around it. Roles without information asymmetry are theatre. | Must |
| FR-AI-10 | **The adversarial reviewer is a governance control, not a productivity feature.** Under C1 the same person forms the hypothesis, chooses the evaluation, sets the thresholds, interprets the result and decides promotion. Tamper-evidence (NFR-08) defeats falsification; it does nothing about confirmation bias. An independent adversarial review — attacking the mechanism, hunting leakage, questioning execution feasibility and surfacing unstated assumptions — is the platform's substitute for the second human the operating model does not have, and is therefore Must rather than Could. | Must |
| FR-AI-11 | No agent may hold credentials or context reaching the FR-VAL-12 sealed holdout, in any role, at any stage. | Must |
| FR-AI-04 | Apply regularisation to AI-generated factors: originality checks against the existing library, complexity limits and hypothesis-alignment checks, to counteract crowding and decay-prone formula mining. | Should |
| FR-AI-05 | AI-assisted monitoring narration: automatic plain-language summaries of drift/decay incidents and review packs, drafted for human review. | Could |
| FR-AI-06 | Batch alpha-mining campaigns (evolutionary/LLM-driven search over factor space) executed on research compute with every candidate logged as a trial, so DSR/PBO statistics reflect the true search breadth (see the open-problem note above). | Could |
| FR-AI-07 | All AI-generated artefacts are labelled as such in the registry with the model, prompt version and generation context recorded; human accountability for every promotion decision is preserved. | Must |
| FR-AI-08 | Parametric look-ahead control: every run involving an LLM records the model identity and its training-data cutoff; no backtest window that precedes the cutoff of any model involved may be labelled out-of-sample, and reports over such windows carry a mandatory parametric-look-ahead annotation. | Must |
| FR-AI-09 | Provide entity and date masking for prompts constructed from historical data (symbols, dates and identifying context pseudonymised) as a mitigation when LLMs are used in historical evaluation, with the masking configuration recorded on the run. | Should |

## 15. Functional Requirements — Hypothesis Registry (FR-HYP)

The hypothesis engine decides *what to research* and, with §20, *what to trade and when*. Its requirements were previously specified separately; they are consolidated here because the engine is part of the platform, not an adjunct to it. Section 21 of the standalone specification's rationale is preserved in the module preambles below — the *why* is what stops a future revision quietly undoing a control.

A **hypothesis** is a falsifiable claim about why a trade should pay, with a stated mechanism and a null. It is not a strategy and not code. The **mechanism** field — *who is on the other side and what are they compensated for* — is what distinguishes an edge from a pattern. Default verification method for this module: test, with inspection for registry records.

**The hypothesis record.** Fields marked locked become immutable once the first gated result exists, per FR-VAL-09: `mechanism`, `claim`, `rejected_mechanisms`, `predictors`, `universe`, `sample` (with epochs spanned), `null`, `role`, `horizon`, `data_frequency`, `latency_sensitivity`, `expected_mean_holding_period`, `evaluation_plan`, `thresholds`, `identification_inputs`. Unlocked: `provenance`, `data_prerequisites`, `status`. Derived, never entered: `trial_count`, read from the ledger per FR-VAL-03.

`role` is one of `signal` (has its own P&L expression), `conditioner` (modulates another trade's sizing or direction), `risk_input` (consumed by other hypotheses or by risk management, never traded), `benchmark`, or `research_question` (mechanism credible, testability on available data is itself the open question — Appendix B's H14 and H23). Appendix B entries carrying "mixed" or "—" are re-roled to the nearest enum value, since the appendix requires every entry to complete this schema and cannot then use values outside it.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-HYP-01 | Reject any hypothesis record whose `mechanism`, `null`, `rejected_mechanisms` or `thresholds` fields are empty. | Must |
| FR-HYP-04 | Where `data_prerequisites` are unmet, status is `blocked_on_data` and the platform refuses gated evaluation against it. | Must |
| FR-HYP-05 | Refuse promotion of a `conditioner` or `risk_input` to standalone live emission. | Must |
| FR-HYP-06 | The registry is searchable by mechanism family, predictor, status and epoch, so a proposal can be checked against prior art before testing begins. | Should |
| FR-HYP-07 | Where a hypothesis declares `identification_inputs` — data or another hypothesis on which its test depends — block gated evaluation until those inputs are available at the stated granularity. | Must |

*`rejected_mechanisms` exists because a hypothesis can otherwise silently carry a mechanism its own evidence contradicts. The seed catalogue's anchor entry did exactly that in an earlier draft: it claimed a preference-based insurance premium while the document's own headline finding argued that channel is approximately zero.*

## 16. Functional Requirements — Hypothesis Exploration (FR-EXP)

Four intake paths — seeded from the Appendix B catalogue, human, literature-derived, machine-generated — all entering the same registry, completing the same schema, traversing the same gates. Provenance changes the decay prior (FR-DEC-06) and nothing else.

**Breadth is priced, not forbidden.** Every variant tested raises the deflated-Sharpe hurdle every survivor must clear (FR-VAL-03). That is what permits unlimited exploration without permitting self-deception. Two consequences are easy to miss: machine-scale search strains the assumption of a countable, honest trial family, and **regime slicing is also search** — testing *s* strategies × *r* regime definitions × *t* thresholds is closer to *s·r·t* effective trials than to *s+r+t*. Default verification method: test.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-EXP-01 | Every hypothesis, regardless of intake path, completes the FR-HYP record schema before any evaluation runs against it. | Must |
| FR-EXP-02 | Each distinct regime definition and each threshold value evaluated increments the trial count for the hypothesis under test. | Must |
| FR-EXP-04 | Before a proposal enters testing, report its similarity to existing registry entries by predictor overlap and mechanism family, so re-discovery is visible. | Should |
| FR-EXP-06 | Detector hyperparameter tuning is search. Sweeping a persistence penalty, feature set or state count increments the trial count of every hypothesis subsequently evaluated against that detector. | Must |
| FR-EXP-07 | **Every machine-driven research campaign is a first-class record**, not a count. A campaign carries: campaign id; the starting hypothesis space; the generating model and version; the prompt or policy version; every candidate produced; the rejection history; the **branching lineage** showing which candidates were conditioned on which earlier results; the stopping rule, declared before the campaign starts; and the final selection. | Must |
| FR-EXP-08 | **Adaptive search invalidates the trial count as a sufficient control, and the platform says so on the output.** The deflated Sharpe corrects for selection from a *fixed family of N trials*; when later candidates are conditioned on earlier results there is no well-defined family, so a larger N does not repair the estimator — it is applied outside its domain. Any evaluation whose lineage shows adaptive conditioning carries a mandatory annotation to that effect, and its DSR is reported as **indicative, not dispositive**. | Must |
| FR-EXP-10 | **Trial counts aggregate across a hypothesis lineage and its mechanism family, not per record.** FR-VAL-03 counts variants "for the hypothesis", but FR-VAL-09's new-hypothesis rule *institutionalises* record-splitting — so a per-record count lets a search be divided until every N is small. The count used by any deflated statistic is the lineage total. | Must |
| FR-EXP-11 | **Cross-charged trials are bounded and decay.** FR-EXP-06 and FR-REG-01b propagate counts forward from shared infrastructure; read literally and without limit, one detector sweep would inflate N for every future hypothesis permanently. Cross-charging applies **only to hypotheses evaluated against that specific artefact version**, and lapses when the artefact is retired or superseded. | Must |
| FR-EXP-09 | **Prior-driven and discovery research run as separate, labelled arms.** One arm may see the Appendix B catalogue; the other proposes mechanisms without it. Comparing their out-of-sample discovery rates is the only way to answer whether machine research *discovers* or merely accelerates the implementation of human priors — and a catalogue given to a generative proposer is an anchor as much as a seed. | Should |

## 17. Functional Requirements — Frequency Scoping (FR-SCOPE)

The platform is mid-frequency: mean holding period in **hours or longer**, individual short holds acceptable but not as the average case. Applying that constraint requires separating three properties routinely conflated:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| **Data frequency** | What sampling granularity is needed to *compute* the signal? |
| **Holding period** | How long is the resulting position held? |
| **Latency sensitivity** | How fast must you act once the signal fires, before the edge decays? |

**This distinction is load-bearing.** FR-DATA-05's variance-premium work requires 5-minute bars to compute realised variance honestly, yet is computed end-of-day and held to expiry: zero latency sensitivity, multi-day hold. Reading "we are not HFT" as "no intraday data" would eliminate the core of the catalogue for no reason. **Intraday sampling is not intraday trading.** Default verification method: test.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-SCOPE-01 | G1 rejects any hypothesis whose `expected_mean_holding_period` is under one hour, or whose `latency_sensitivity` is `high`, unless an explicit recorded exemption is granted at registration. The one-hour floor is deliberately looser than the "hours or longer" constraint: this is the coarse per-hypothesis filter, and FR-SCOPE-02's book-level control is the real constraint. A single fast hypothesis is harmless; a book that drifts intraday is not. | Must |
| FR-SCOPE-02 | Monitor the live book's **realised** holding-period distribution and alert on breach. The primary measure is the **share of trades held below the floor**, with median as secondary — a mean floor is trivially gamed by a barbell of many short trades plus one long position, which passes while the book is operationally intraday. | Must |
| FR-SCOPE-03 | Where a hypothesis requires intraday data purely as offline *sampling*, record that distinctly from a requirement for intraday *execution*, so data-acquisition decisions are not justified against the wrong axis. | Should |

## 18. Functional Requirements — Regime (FR-REG)

**Regime and epoch are different things and must never be conflated.** A regime is a statistical state the market moves between — a parameter vector (drift, volatility, downside deviation), not a narrative label. An **epoch** (FR-DATA-16) is a change in market structure or participant composition, invisible to any return-based detector.

**The hardest constraint in this module.** At persistence settings that avoid whipsaw, five years of history contains roughly **two to three regime episodes in total** — not per regime. At settings producing many episodes, turnover destroys the edge. Persistence and statistical power are in direct opposition and five years cannot supply both. Everything below follows from that. Default verification method: test.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-REG-01 | **Before the regime subsystem is built**, run and record a horse race on identical forward P&L comparing (a) a smooth function of the continuous conditioning variable, (b) **the filtered-probability weight the design actually specifies**, (c) the discrete label as reference only, and (d) a trend conditioner as the practitioner-standard rival. Decision metric is pre-registered and **tail-based**, per FR-GATE-02: (b) must reduce maximum drawdown and conditional VaR at matched return, out of sample, after costs. **If it does not, the regime layer is not built** and the platform implements continuous conditioning instead. | Must |
| FR-REG-01a | The forward-P&L series is the backtest P&L of a pre-registered, fixed set of naive benchmarks (FR-GATE-16) — not candidate signals, whose selection would contaminate the comparison. | Must |
| FR-REG-01b | The horse race's own multiplicity is charged downstream: selecting among conditioning technologies shapes every subsequent regime-conditioned evaluation, so it increments the trial count of **every hypothesis later evaluated against the winning conditioner**. Charging it only to benchmarks whose counts gate nothing would let the subsystem's most consequential model-selection decision escape the ledger. | Must |
| FR-REG-02 | Cap regime state count at 3; default 2. A four-state univariate Gaussian model carries 20 free parameters, several of which would rest on ~2 effective observations at realistic sample sizes. | Must — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-03 | Report **episode-count n**, never day-count n, on every per-regime statistic, and visually suppress any statistic backed by fewer than 5 episodes. | Must — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-04 | Resample with a stationary or block bootstrap at block length comparable to dwell time. IID day-level resampling produces confidence intervals roughly √(dwell) too narrow. | Must — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-05 | **The prefix-property test.** Compute the regime series through *T*, recompute through *T+k*, assert element-wise equality on the overlapping prefix. A failing detector is barred from every backtest path. This single assertion catches smoothed decoding, full-sample scalers, full-sample quantile thresholds and forward-filled features at once, and it runs in CI. **Checked at G1** — it is a property of the detector, not of any hypothesis's results. | Must |
| FR-REG-06 | Regime thresholds are computed from trailing windows only, never full-sample quantiles — the same defect as smoothed labels in different clothing, and easier to commit by accident. | Must — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-07 | Alert when a regime-conditional strategy's Sharpe exceeds buy-and-hold by more than approximately 2×; that ratio is the observed signature of the smoothed-label defect. *Heuristic tripwire derived from practitioner-tier evidence, not a validated threshold.* | Should — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-08 | Hard position limits and defined-risk structures sit **beneath** the regime layer, enforced per FR-ENF. A regime signal is never the primary tail control for a short-gamma position: detection latency is reported at median 2–3 days and 90th percentile ~7 days, which for Indian weekly options is most of a contract's life. | Must |
| FR-REG-09 | The operator-facing threshold control displays its measured false-positive rate at selection time. | Should — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-10 | Persistence is an explicit tunable hyperparameter; switches-per-year is a first-class diagnostic on every run. | Must — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-11 | After every retrain, re-map state indices to a canonical ordering and log a diff of the trailing 250 days' labels. A large diff means the detector changed, not the market. | Should — *contingent* |
| FR-REG-12 | Epoch data is owned by the FR-DATA-16 registry — verified registry data, not a hard-coded calendar. This module adds only the behaviour: **refuse training windows spanning an epoch boundary** for any expiry-day or short-gamma hypothesis, or for the regime detector, without recorded justification. | Must |
| FR-REG-13 | Declare and record the detector's retrain cadence, training window, and whether training windows may span epoch boundaries (they may not, per FR-REG-12). | Must — *contingent* |

**Recommended detection method**, for the design that follows: a statistical jump model with an explicit persistence penalty, or Bayesian online change-point detection — whose causality is structural, there being no smoothed variant to use by accident. Markov-switching GARCH is a poor first build; its path-dependence problem makes exact likelihood computation infeasible, so it imports an estimation research problem rather than a library call. Features: 3–5 return-derived exponentially-weighted measures at mixed halflives, plus the variance risk premium. No macro, correlation or liquidity inputs in the first version — every added feature multiplies the trial count under FR-EXP-06.

| FR-GATE-13 | For any hypothesis whose P&L expression is a non-linear option payoff, G3 assesses significance against a simulated null. Calibrating to option prices embeds the variance premium in the fitted parameters, so the null contains the effect under test and **the test can never reject, silently, with no error and no diagnostic**. Option prices for the null are generated under zero risk premia. **Parameter uncertainty is propagated** via a parameter bootstrap, not a point estimate. **The null model is itself validated** — family adequacy and moment matching against the index's realised distribution — because a misspecified null rejects or accepts with exactly the silent, diagnostic-free failure this requirement warns about for price calibration. **A declared fallback applies if the null proves unbuildable in the phase timebox:** the gate falls back to the FR-GATE-17 benchmark increment alone, and the limitation is recorded on every affected result rather than the requirement being quietly dropped. | Must |

| FR-GATE-17 | G3 evaluates the candidate's increment over its declared benchmark against a threshold locked at G0. ## 20. Functional Requirements — The Allocator (FR-ALLOC)

Which live signals run, at what weight, and what happens when two conflict. **New runtime scope relative to FRD v0.2** — §1.2 is amended accordingly.

**Design position: deliberately dumb, but specified.** An allocator sits downstream of every anti-overfitting control in the platform, where nothing is watching it. The evidence for sophistication is weak: the disagreement in the volatility-managed-portfolio literature is not about whether volatility is forecastable but about whether **the conditioning-to-weight mapping is stable enough to estimate in real time**. Dumb, however, is not the same as vague. Default verification method: test.

**Why margin contribution and not expected shortfall.** Equal expected-shortfall contribution requires a joint tail model across every live signal — estimator choice, dependence structure, tail assumptions — for a short-gamma book whose tail is precisely the thing there is no data on. SPAN-style margin is externally computed by the exchange, conservative, stress-based rather than historically estimated, and already the binding constraint under FR-CAP-01. It requires zero estimation.

**The caveat that makes it safe: margin-efficient is not low-risk.** Far-out-of-the-money short options are margin-cheap and gap-heavy, so equal-margin weighting over-allocates to precisely the structures margin under-prices. Equal-margin allocation is valid **only in combination with** FR-CAP-03's concentration limits — the moneyness dimension especially — and FR-GATE-04's gamma cap. Those are mandatory complements, not optional overlays.

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-ALLOC-01 | **v1 is fixed capital allocation plus hard risk caps — nothing more — until at least two independently validated signals exist (§25).** Once they do, v1.1 allocates by **equal margin contribution** within a stated margin budget, with FR-CAP-03 concentration limits and the FR-GATE-04 gamma cap enforced as preconditions rather than later checks. Expected-shortfall contribution is deferred to v2 under FR-ALLOC-04. If FR-REG-01 concludes the regime layer is not built, the continuous conditioning variable substitutes for the regime multiplier in FR-ALLOC-06's role and nothing else changes. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-02 | Conflict is evaluated **per exposure axis** — delta, vega, gamma — not on a single undefined "direction". Two signals conflict only where they oppose on the same axis, over the same underlying, in an overlapping window, and are not in a declared relationship. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-04 | Any allocator more sophisticated than v1 is estimated on a trailing window, applied forward, and reported only on that basis — never fitted on the full sample and reported in-sample. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-05 | Review the conflict log periodically; a persistently conflicting undeclared pair indicates at least one hypothesis is mis-specified. | Should |
| FR-ALLOC-06 | The regime exposure multiplier applies **by exposure class, not uniformly**: it scales short-volatility and short-gamma exposure toward flat, leaves long-volatility and declared hedge exposure unscaled, and **never scales any exposure up**. Applied uniformly it would de-risk defensive positions exactly when they should be held. Each signal declares its exposure class at registration. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-07 | Signals may declare a **relationship** — `hedge`, `overlay`, `pair` — at registration. Declared relationships are exempt from the conflict veto, and the declaration is part of the locked record so it cannot be added after a conflict surfaces. Without this, a long-put tail hedge permanently opposes the core short-volatility book on the vega axis and the veto would cancel both the hedge and what it hedges. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-08 | G6 models **suppression interactions**: promoting a signal that would frequently veto an incumbent on shared underlyings is a cost to the book, and the incremental-contribution check accounts for it. Otherwise a new signal can silently silence a better incumbent. | Should |
| FR-ALLOC-09 | Rebalancing is **band-triggered, not continuous**: the book re-sizes only when the target weight departs from the live weight by more than a configured band, with a minimum dwell between rebalances. Band and dwell are versioned configuration. Every options re-weight is a close-and-reopen at spread, so continuous re-weighting either bleeds cost or gets silently discretised by the implementer. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-10 | The cost of a rebalance is modelled as unwind at prevailing spreads **plus** reopen at prevailing spreads, never as a notional delta, and the backtest survives **the upper credible bound of FR-BT-15's cost envelope** on that basis — not a fixed multiplier, which FR-BT-15 abolishes as a guess about uncertainty the model measures directly. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-11 | The regime multiplier is registered as a **risk control** with its own record, distinct from the hypothesis registry, recording its classification, the asymmetry argument below, and the FR-ALLOC-06 no-upward-scaling condition on which the classification depends. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-12 | Its evidence standard is explicitly **lower than G4's and explicitly stated**: FR-REG-01's horse race showing drawdown reduction at matched return, plus the FR-GATE-05 corruption test, but **not** the five-episode floor, which FR-GATE-15 establishes is unreachable. The waiver is recorded, not implied. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-13 | **Pre-registered removal criteria**, locked before deployment: the multiplier is reviewed **quarterly, and additionally on every epoch boundary**, against cumulative foregone premium versus avoided drawdown — epochs alone are irregular structural events potentially years apart, which would leave the control dormant exactly when it is accumulating cost, and removed if foregone premium exceeds avoided drawdown by a locked margin over a locked window. A precautionary control that never pays for itself is a cost, not a control. | Must |
| FR-ALLOC-14 | The multiplier is **monitored live in its own right** — FR-MON-09 covers signals and nothing otherwise watches the multiplier. Reporting shows continuously the premium foregone to de-risking and the drawdown avoided, so the FR-ALLOC-13 review is a reading rather than a reconstruction. | Must |

**Why the multiplier is exempt from G4, stated rather than assumed.** FR-GATE-15 establishes that regime-conditional claims are untestable on available history — yet FR-ALLOC-06 applies a regime-conditioned multiplier across the entire live book, which is the same claim class at the widest possible blast radius. The resolution is that the multiplier is classified as a **precautionary risk control, not a return claim**, on an asymmetric loss function: a false de-risk costs bounded foregone premium; a missed de-risk costs a gap loss on a short-gamma book.

**This argument is available only because FR-ALLOC-06 forbids scaling up.** A multiplier that only ever reduces exposure cannot manufacture returns; its worst case is opportunity cost. **If any future revision permits upward scaling, the classification is void and the multiplier becomes an alpha claim subject to G4 like everything else.** That conditionality is the entire basis of the exemption and must not be quietly dropped.

### 21.1 The identity model — who may invoke a control

Several controls in this document were specified as boundaries without saying **who is on either side**: the information boundaries between AI roles, the sealed holdout, and the operator API that promotes, rolls back and kills. Each was written as though OS-user separation covered it. It does not — **OS-user separation is void over HTTP on localhost**, and it says nothing about two processes running as the same user.

**The hard problem, named.** Under C1 there is one human, so authenticating *people* is trivial and useless. The real distinction is between **the operator and the operator's agents** — autonomous, numerous, running continuously, and required by FR-AI-02/03 to act through the SDK and registry rather than around them. An unauthenticated API is an around-path, and the platform cannot tell an agent's call from the operator's.

**Principals.** Deliberately a short capability list, not the entitlement subsystem §1.2.1 excludes:

| Principal | Runs as | May |
|---|---|---|
| `operator` | interactive session | Everything, including promotion, rollback, kill and threshold locking |
| `research-agent` | research slice | Read research zones, submit jobs, write to the registry through the SDK. **Never** promote, roll back, kill, lock thresholds or read the holdout |
| `worker` | research slice | Claim and submit jobs, read snapshots, write results |
| `capture` | capture slice | Write raw and derived zones and the online feature store |
| `promotion` | capture slice | Read the holdout, write gate records |
| `backup` | own identity | Read everything, write only to the backup target |

| ID | Requirement | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| FR-ENF-06 | Every control declares the **principals permitted to invoke it**, default deny. A control without a declared principal set is not deployable. | Must |
| FR-ENF-07 | **The operator API authenticates, on localhost as everywhere else.** Promotion, rollback, kill-switch and threshold-locking endpoints require a credential bound to the `operator` principal. OS-user separation does not reach across HTTP and must not be relied on to. | Must |
| FR-ENF-08 | **The operator credential is structurally unavailable to research-slice processes** — held outside the research slice and supplied per session, not stored where an agent running as the research user can read it. This is what distinguishes the operator from the operator's agents; without it FR-AI-02/03's "through the SDK, never around it" is unenforceable and FR-AI-03's information boundaries are, in this document's own words, theatre. | Must |
| FR-ENF-09 | **Information boundaries between AI roles are enforced by the platform, not by prompt discipline.** A role that must not see out-of-sample results is served a context the platform constructs and filters; it is not given registry read access and asked not to look. **Where a boundary cannot be enforced, the role is not claimed to have it.** | Must |
| FR-ENF-10 | **Holdout reads are recorded in the tamper-evident ledger and anchored offsite** on the NFR-08 schedule. FR-FB covers the feedback loop and FR-MON covers signal-level decay. This module covers what neither does: monitoring the **mechanism** rather than the P&L. A premium can erode because its source is competed or legislated away, and that erosion is visible in the premium series long before it appears in any signal's drawdown. Default verification method: test.

## 24. Minimal Credible Core
The Minimal Credible Core (MCC) is the smallest subset of this document sufficient to run one honest experiment end to end — smaller than Phase 1, and the thing actually worth building first.

**Membership test, applied rather than assumed.** A requirement belongs in the MCC only if it satisfies one of two conditions:

1. **The loop does not close without it.** Data → hypothesis → signal → realistic backtest → out-of-sample test → decision. Anything not on that path waits.
2. **It cannot be retrofitted.** Prospective capture (FR-DATA-14) is irrecoverable — a month not captured is gone. The trial ledger is the same: trials that went unrecorded cannot be counted later, and every deflated statistic computed afterwards is wrong by an unknowable amount. Locked thresholds (FR-VAL-09) cannot be locked retroactively. The sealed holdout (FR-VAL-12) cannot be sealed after it has been read.

**A third condition, added after review.** A requirement is also in the MCC if the *experiment is dishonest without it*, even where the loop mechanically closes. Data QC (FR-DATA-08) and the epoch registry with annotated reporting (FR-DATA-16, FR-BT-12) qualify: any first experiment on 2023–2026 index-option history **necessarily spans the October and November 2024 and September 2025 structural breaks**, and FR-BT-12 bars silent pooling across them. An MCC that pools across the most disruptive breaks in the data, on un-QC'd chains, produces a result the platform's own rules forbid trusting. The loop closing and the answer meaning something are different tests.

**The test matters because the conditions pull in opposite directions**, and only the second justifies building integrity machinery before knowing whether an edge exists at all. Controls that *can* be added later — hash-chaining an existing ledger, offsite backup, full margin modelling, vendor-supplied Greeks in place of computed ones — should be, and are moved out of the MCC accordingly. The risk this test guards against is real and specific: **treating infrastructure completeness as progress**, and building a research operating system before establishing that the loop it serves can answer whether a credible, executable edge exists in Indian F&O at all.

The MCC comprises: point-in-time storage with as-of queries and snapshots for one index underlying's options chain (FR-DATA-01/03/09 restricted in scope), prospective capture running from day one (FR-DATA-14/15), basic IV and first-order Greeks (FR-DATA-05 core), the hypothesis registry and auto-logged experiment tracking with the exploration boundary (FR-RES-03/04/09), one signal implemented against the contract in backtest mode (FR-SIG-02/03 minimal), the backtester with the full cost stack, expiry settlement and participation caps but simplified margin (FR-BT-01/02/03/04/09, FR-CAP-04), walk-forward plus DSR/PBO with locked thresholds (FR-VAL-01/03/04/05/09), append-only ledgers **with hash-chaining from the first row** — NFR-08 is *not* deferred after all: retrofitted chaining protects future rows and not rows edited before chaining began, so locking thresholds (FR-VAL-09) in a mutable store during the MCC would make MCC-era locking convention rather than construction, in a period fully controlled by the threat model's primary adversary. Offsite backup (NFR-07) remains retrofittable and is deferred, and **the sealed holdout carved and sealed before any research begins (FR-VAL-12/13)** — which is in the MCC purely on the irretrofittable test, since a holdout cannot be sealed after it has been looked at.

Everything else — CPCV, the feature store, surface fitting, forward testing, monitoring, the regime subsystem, the allocator beyond fixed weights, and AI assistance — layers on after the MCC has produced its first gated result.
## 25. Phasing and Effort
Phase 1 (foundation, the MoSCoW timebox): the MCC plus the remaining Phase 1 Musts — full margin modelling, CPCV, the feature store, epoch registry and epoch-annotated reporting, sizing policy as artefact, and data QC. Phase 2 (lifecycle): FR-FWD, FR-DEP, FR-MON (including pre-registered kill criteria), FR-FB. Phase 3 (leverage): FR-AI, remaining Should/Could items, and FR-EXE to close the loop into xman.

**Two subsystems are sequenced by evidence rather than by phase**, because building either before its precondition is met would be researching the wrong question:

- **The allocator (§20) is gated on having at least two independently validated signals.** Until then it is **fixed capital allocation plus hard risk caps** and nothing more. "How should capital be allocated between signals?" cannot be answered — and should not be architected — before "do two genuinely independent signals exist?" has been. FR-ALLOC-02 and 05–14 are therefore contingent on that precondition, not on a calendar phase. The risk is concrete: an allocator is sophisticated enough to become a research problem in its own right, and it sits downstream of every anti-overfitting control where nothing is watching it.
- **The regime subsystem (§18) is Phase 2 at the earliest, and only if a concrete signal demonstrates it needs it.** FR-REG-01's horse race already establishes that it may not be built at all. Beyond that, a regime layer applied early risks converting a reasonably robust *unconditional* signal into a heavily parameterised *conditional* one with far less statistical power — spending the scarce resource (episodes, §18) to buy conditioning nobody has shown is needed.
Effort is stated as bands with the capacity assumption named, per the review. For a small team (2–3 engineers): MCC in roughly 6–10 weeks, Phase 1 in 3–4 months, full lifecycle in 6–9 months. For a single practitioner without AI assistance: MCC in roughly 3–5 months, Phase 1 approaching a year, and the earlier headline estimates would be misleading. The operative assumption for this platform (constraint C1) is a single practitioner with substantial agentic AI development capacity, which experience suggests sits between the bands — closer to the small-team band for well-specified components (backtester mechanics, storage, CI) and closer to the solo band for judgement-heavy components (cost-model calibration, validation-gate design, epoch analysis). These bands are planning inputs, not commitments, and should be revised after the MCC lands.
## 26. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point-in-time (PIT) | Data as it was known at a given moment, excluding later revisions; the prerequisite for lookahead-free research. |
| CPCV | Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation: multiple train/test path combinations with purging and embargoing of overlapping/serially-correlated samples (López de Prado). |
| DSR | Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Sharpe corrected for number of trials, selection bias and non-normal returns (Bailey & López de Prado, 2014). |
| PBO | Probability of Backtest Overfitting: likelihood the selected configuration outperformed in-sample by chance (Bailey et al., 2016). |
| MinBTL / MinTRL | Minimum backtest length / minimum track-record length: computable sample-size floors below which an apparent result at a given trial count is expected by chance. |
| Feature store | Registry serving identical feature computations to offline (research) and online (production) consumers, preventing training/serving skew. |
| SVI / SSVI / SABR | Parametric implied-volatility smile/surface models used for arbitrage-free surface fitting and interpolation. |
| VRP | Variance risk premium: implied minus realised volatility, a core options signal input. |
| Champion/challenger | Running a candidate version alongside the production incumbent on identical inputs for statistically grounded comparison. |
| Signal decay | Erosion of a signal's predictive power over time through crowding, regime change or overfit discovery. |
| Training/serving skew | Divergence between feature values computed in research and in production for the same inputs. |
| Parametric look-ahead | An LLM's knowledge of historical outcomes from its training data contaminating a backtest over a period before its training cutoff. |
| Regulatory epoch | A named period of stable market structure between structural regulatory changes; results must not silently pool across epoch boundaries. |
| Cost-breakeven multiple | The multiplier on modelled total costs at which a strategy's net expectancy reaches zero; a fragility measure. |

## Appendix B — Seed Hypothesis Catalogue

The canon a hypothesis engine starts from, before anyone invents anything. Reference material rather than requirements: these are *inputs* to the platform, not obligations on it. Each is a candidate registry entry and must complete the FR-HYP schema before testing.

**Evidence grades.** **A** replicated peer-reviewed with at least partial Indian evidence · **B** strong peer-reviewed, US/EU only, transfer to India is an assumption · **C** contested, or practitioner/vendor tier · **D** mechanism plausible, evidence absent.

**The finding that should change priors before reading the rest.** Equity index option alphas in the US have become statistically indistinguishable from zero over roughly the past fifteen years, while synthetic options constructed to strip out option-market frictions have never shown negative alpha across a century. The implication is that the variance risk premium was substantially an **intermediation rent** rather than a deep preference-based risk premium, and it eroded as frictions fell. *(Source is a 2025 working paper, not peer-reviewed; direction is corroborated by the authors' earlier work but the VRP-decline literature is not unanimous.)*

**Where the India transfer needs care.** That model's friction is a *structural* inability of retail to sell options. **No such constraint exists in India** — Indian retail can and demonstrably does write options at scale. What India plausibly has is a large *net* demand imbalance in short-dated index options, which is a behavioural fact rather than a binding constraint. The argument therefore runs through the demand-pressure and limits-to-arbitrage channel (H4's mechanism), yielding a weaker but still falsifiable prediction: if Indian premia are substantially an imbalance rent, the measured Indian premium should exceed the current US premium and should decline as the imbalance declines. **That requires measuring the imbalance, not assuming it** — H12 is the identification input.

### Anchor hypotheses

| ID | Claim | Grade | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | Implied variance systematically exceeds subsequently realised variance; selling variance earns a premium | A | signal |
| **H2** | The variance risk premium level predicts subsequent index excess returns, strongest at roughly quarterly horizon — **not** a weekly-expiry timing signal | B | conditioner |
| **H23** | Indian premia are substantially an imbalance rent; the premium should decline as the net retail imbalance declines | C | research question |

**H1's mechanism** is the **intermediation rent**: one side of the market persistently demands convexity, and capacity-constrained intermediaries absorb it, compensated for inventory risk they cannot fully hedge, for jump exposure dynamic hedging cannot replicate, and for margin that binds precisely when the payout occurs.

**H1's rejected mechanism** — recorded because a hypothesis must not silently carry a mechanism its own evidence contradicts — is the deep preference-based story, that buyers purchase insurance against a wealth-destroying state so it prices above actuarial value. If frictionless synthetic options never showed negative alpha across a century, that channel is approximately zero and cannot be the source.

**H1's critical refinement**, from the one verified India study: only past **continuous** variance forecasts variance-swap returns — **realised jumps have no predictive power** — so a jump-loaded realised-variance estimator degrades the signal. Separate the components.

**Role counts:** nine signals (H1, H4, H9, H10, H15b, H17, H18, H21, H26) · six conditioners (H2, H7, H8-inversion, H11, H12, H27) · three risk inputs (H20, H22, H25) · two research questions (H14, H23) · two out of scope on frequency grounds (H15a, and the withdrawn surface-relative-value entry).

**Backlog, recorded not specified:** VRP term structure (needs multi-expiry) · risk-neutral skewness (**the literature's sign conventions are genuinely split and the two common measures move in opposite directions**; any implementation must store the definition with the series) · tail-hedging cost (subsumed by H25) · PCR and max pain (**max pain has no independent mechanism**; carry only as a benchmark) · expiry-week theta (re-indexed by the Sep 2025 migration) · expiry-day migration as a natural experiment (**the best available way to validate the gamma and expiry families causally**).

### Two inference errors the catalogue must actively prevent

**"Retail loses money" does not imply "fading retail pays."** SEBI's FY25 study reports ~91% of individual F&O traders losing money and aggregate net losses around ₹1.06 lakh crore. But those losses are substantially **transaction costs, STT, spreads and adverse selection** — symmetric frictions a systematic fader also pays, not transfers to whoever takes the other side. **No study was found demonstrating a strategy profiting by fading retail positioning.** Any hypothesis invoking this must address the frictions objection in its `mechanism` field.

**High IV rank does not imply cheap-to-sell.** The evidence is vendor-tier and measures win rate and premium collected — the wrong denominators. The discriminating question is P&L **per unit of realised gamma risk or maximum adverse excursion**. And the variance premium *compresses* after volatility spikes, exactly when IV rank peaks, because realised volatility catches up. Prefer the variance risk premium, which has peer-reviewed forward-predictive support, over IV rank, which does not.

### Evidence gaps the platform must fill itself

No Indian dealer-gamma study. No Indian net-buying-pressure study. No credible modern Indian pinning study. No Indian dispersion measurement. No systematic treatment of the Indian index-level event set — despite the Union Budget being among the largest scheduled volatility events in any equity market. No Indian 0DTE literature, in arguably the world's largest short-dated options market.

**Nearly the entire catalogue is US or European evidence whose transfer to India is an explicit assumption.** The only genuinely India-based evidence verified is one 2020 study of the Indian variance risk premium, the SEBI regulatory record, and a 2025 study of the expiry-day change.
